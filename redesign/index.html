Why it matters

Pollution is a public-health story hiding in plain sight

Air pollution is linked to roughly one in nine deaths worldwide. The World Health Organization attributes about seven million premature deaths every year to it — around 4.2 million from ambient (outdoor) pollution and 3.8 million from household air.

Yet across much of Africa the data needed to act on this — street-level, hour-by-hour, independently verifiable — barely exists. sensors.AFRICA closes that gap by putting low-cost, open-source sensors in the hands of citizens, then publishing what they find as open data anyone can interrogate: a journalist chasing a lead, a parent choosing a school route, a city official defending a budget.

How it works

A weather station for pollution, on your wall

1

Get a sensor

We use open-source Luftdaten technology built around the SDS011 particulate sensor — cheap enough for a household, accurate enough for a newsroom.

2

Mount it & connect

Fix it outside a window or on a balcony, connect it to Wi-Fi, and it starts measuring PM2.5 and PM10 in the air around your home.

3

Your data goes public

Readings stream to the open sensors.AFRICA platform — mapped, archived and free for journalists, researchers and neighbours to use.

Your city can't fix what it can't see.

Host a sensor at your home, school or office and put your street on the map. No lab coat required — just a window ledge and Wi-Fi.
